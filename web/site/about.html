About Wynkyn

A place to write, lay out and print a book you will bind by hand.

Who it is for

Anyone who has wanted to format a high quality book. Whether you hand bind books, write a family history or draft your own book, you can write, edit and typeset it in one place.

Until now the choice has been a professional layout program, which costs money every month and takes weeks to learn, or a word processor, which has severely limited book-centered formatting and printing options. Wynkyn is absolutely free to use, while providing invaluable book formatting, setting and printing functionality.

What it does

You write and edit the manuscript in Wynkyn, in plain text with obvious markers for book features like chapters, epigraphs, poems, pull quotes, sidebars, drop caps and footnotes. Styling allows you to add running headers, customize page numbers, use Google Fonts, adjust spacing, customize drop caps and much more. Your table of contents is automatically synced as you experiment with fonts, kerning, indents and line height. Wynkyn lays the book out in your browser with Paged.js and shows the real pages as you write: the same pagination the printer gets, so there is no surprise at the end.

For publishing, Wynkyn gives you all the options you need for hand-bound books, like paper dimensions, signature sizes, folds and cuts. You print, export to PDF or EPUB as many times as you'd like.

Your books are yours

Wynkyn never reads, analyses, trains on, shares or sells your manuscripts, and never will. There is no backend server. In Chromium-based browsers, like Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge, you can work from a folder on your own computer. Your manuscripts are saved to your device, easy to find, simple to share, and you will never need a third-party program to read them.

Built on Paged.js

None of this would be possible without Paged.js, the open-source library that turns HTML into paged, printable books inside a browser. Running heads, folios, footnotes, page breaks that fall where a book needs them: Paged.js does the hard part, and Wynkyn is a small set of tools built on top of it. It is founded by Adam Hyde and maintained by Julien Taquet, Fred Chasen and Gijs de Heij, and it is free to use. If Wynkyn earns a place on your shelf, they are the people to thank. Their GitHub page is where the project takes sponsorship, and the best place to support it.

Free and open source

Wynkyn is MIT licensed. The source is on GitHub, where it can also run as a local app with a command-line export that renders print-ready PDFs. Everything you can do on your own device is free and will stay free.

The name

Wynkyn de Worde is credited with popularizing printing in England. He was trained by William Caxton, who brought the first printing press to the country in 1476. After Caxton's death, de Worde took over the printing press at Westminster. He believed printing was not just a novelty for courts, governments, the church and the wealthy, but it could be done cheaply and brought to the masses. Around 1500 he opened his print shop on Fleet Street in London, and over the years the street became the home of English printing. From scripture to romances to newspapers, printing became mainstream.

De Worde's mark was a sun. Five hundred years later, you can still see his sun printed in his surviving books.
