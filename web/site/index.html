Write, typeset and print a book to bind by hand. Free, from your own files.

Write and edit the manuscript in Wynkyn, in plain text. The finished pages appear beside it as you type, laid out exactly as they will print. When the book is ready, Wynkyn arranges the pages on each sheet so the folded sheets nest into signatures, the gatherings a hand-bound book is sewn from. It also exports an EPUB for e-readers.

Open the editor No account. Nothing to install. Works best in Chrome or Edge.

  • Free and open source. MIT licensed, no tiers, no trial.
  • No account. Open the editor and start writing.
  • No backend server. Everything runs in your browser. A book is a folder of plain files on your own computer, never uploaded.
  • Print at home. Fold and nest the printed sheets and the signatures read in order, ready to sew. Or export an EPUB for e-readers.

Why it exists

Software for making a book comes in two kinds. The professional layout programs, InDesign and the like, cost a fee every month, take weeks to learn, and still know nothing about folding a sheet into a signature. Word processors and writing apps are easy to pick up but are fall short: limited running heads, no drop caps, no say over where a chapter opens, and nothing that turns pages into printable sheets. Wynkyn is both free and give book-centered functionality.

How it works

Wynkyn has three views: Write, where you edit the manuscript; Style, where you shape how the pages look; and Publish, where you print or export. A book is a folder: manuscript.md holds the words with a few plain markers (chapters, epigraphs, poems, footnotes), and metadata.json holds every styling decision. The app keeps them apart, so you never put a font or a page break in your prose.

Styling gives you unlimited options and even allows you to add custom CSS. Change the rule for chapter titles once and every chapter follows. The preview is the same layout the printer gets, so what you see is what you fold. The pagination itself is the work of Paged.js, the open-source library Wynkyn is built on; without it there would be no Wynkyn.
